# Research Brief: Harmonic Language of Joe Pass, Stevie Wonder, Leon Thomas, D'Angelo
**Date:** 2026-03-08
**Depth:** deep
**Rounds:** 4
**Project:** WESLEYFRANKLIN / Scales.sin

---

## TL;DR
All four artists share a core vocabulary of extended minor/dominant harmony (min7, min9, min11, dom7b9, maj9, sus4/sus2) deployed over modal vamps rather than functional ii-V-I resolution — but each arrives there from a different tradition: bebop voice-leading (Pass), Dorian/Mixolydian funk (Wonder), gospel chord clusters (D'Angelo), and contemporary R&B layering (Thomas).

---

## Recommendation
For Scales.sin, prioritize these five teaching/visualization modules, in this order:

1. **Modal vamps** — Dorian and Mixolydian two-chord vamps (i–IV, I–bVII) as the foundational groove environment. Every artist lives here.
2. **Extended minor chord voicings** — m7, m9, m11 as a chord family with visual note-addition progression. The min11 is the shared apex chord across all four.
3. **The sus4 / 13sus family** — teach dom13sus4 as "the neo-soul dominant" that replaces functional V7 resolution. Critical for D'Angelo and Thomas; appears in Wonder's cadences too.
4. **Chromatic parallelism** — Wonder's descending 9th chord runs and Pass's chromatic approach chord pairs. Great for showing how chromaticism works in both jazz and pop/soul.
5. **Tritone substitution** — Pass is the clearest entry point. Visualize bII7 substituting for V7 with shared tritone interval highlighted.

Secondary build: A "style fingerprint" feature — given a chord progression input, show which of these four artists it most resembles based on the rule-sets below.

## Key Findings by Artist

### Joe Pass

**Foundational approach:**
Pass's entire system rests on one rule: for every chord shape, know the scale and arpeggio under it simultaneously. This is directly teachable in Scales.sin — chord-scale pairing visualization is native to his method [1][2].

**Voicing types he used:**
- Drop 2 and Drop 3 voicings for chord-melody harmonization [1][3]
- Rootless voicings (root, 5th, or 3rd must be in the bass unless walking through a chord melody passage) [4]
- Two-note to six-note voicing range; six-note voicings for maximum richness [4]
- Virtually all altered tones and extensions sit on the top strings — bass clarity rule [5]

**Chord types and extensions:**
- Minor 11ths, Dominant 13ths, stacked alterations on dominant chords [5]
- For dominant chords: ascending melodic minor scale a semitone above the root generates the altered extensions (b9, #11, b13) [5]
- Minor ii V I: over Dm7b5 → G7alt, moves Dm7b5 voicing up a minor 3rd to Fm7b5 — a signature substitution move [2]

**Tritone substitution:**
- Db7(b5) as tritone sub for G7: swaps bass note a tritone away, allows bass to resolve down a semitone [3]
- Ab13sus4 → Ab13 → G13sus4 → G13 → Gb13sus4 → Gb13 → F13sus4 → F13: chromatic descending 13sus4 → 13 resolution chains [2]

**Approach chords:**
- Chromatic approach from above or below the target chord root using dominant voicings [4]
- Diminished 7th chord functions: moves in minor 3rd intervals (same fingering up 3 frets); dominant 7th chords with "diminished character" (7b9, 7b5, 13b9, 7#5b9) share this minor 3rd symmetry [4]
- Minor 7th chord substituting for dominant 7th: Dm7 standing in for G7 (related chord substitution) [6]

**Harmonic fingerprint:**
Pass simplifies to three chord qualities internally (major, minor, dominant) then decorates. His chord book philosophy: voicings are meaningless without melodic context. Melody first, always.

**Core progressions:**
```
ii V I (major): Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7 (enriched with drop2/drop3 rootless voicings) [7]
ii V I (minor): Dm7b5 | G7alt (Fm7b5 voicing) | Cm(maj7)
Chromatic descent: Ab13sus4–Ab13–G13sus4–G13–Gb13sus4–Gb13–F13sus4–F13
```

**Best reference resources:**
- *Joe Pass Guitar Style* (Alfred) — Part 1: Harmony (chord construction, embellishment, substitution, connection, symmetric chords); Part 2: Melody [8]
- *Joe Pass Guitar Chords* (Alfred) — companion chord diagram book [8]
- Young-Soo Kim, "An Analysis of the Solo Guitar Style of Joe Pass" (University of Pittsburgh doctoral thesis) — academic analysis, PDF at modernguitarharmony.com [9]
- Pablo Records *Virtuoso* series (1973-1977) — primary listening reference for chord-melody application

### Stevie Wonder

**Modal foundation:**
Wonder's groove tracks are predominantly modal — Dorian and Mixolydian. This is the anchor, not functional I-IV-V [10].

- "I Wish": Eb Dorian. Two-chord vamp: i–IV (Ebm → Abmaj7). Bridge: V–VI–ii–iv–V–VI–ii–V in Eb Dorian [10]
- "Superstition": Eb Dorian. Single dominant vamp — harmonic stasis, groove-driven [10]
- "Higher Ground": Eb Dorian (same tonal center as the above two — Wonder returns to this tonal region repeatedly) [10]

**Chromatic parallelism (signature move):**
"Sir Duke" pre-chorus: chromatic descending/ascending run of 9th chords used as passing chords. Full sequence: E9–Eb9–D9–Db9 (descending), D9–D#9–E9 (ascending back), capped with F9 (bV tritone) resolving to F#9 (V) [11]. This is Wonder's most technically unusual harmonic gesture — purely chromatic, diatonically disorienting, resolved by tempo.

**Modal interchange / borrowed chords:**
Wonder is described uniformly as "an actual modal interchange king" [12].
- IV → IVm (major to parallel minor IV chord): "Lately" uses Gb minor borrowed from parallel minor [12]
- bVII major chord borrowed from Mixolydian: "My Cherie Amour" uses Cb major (bVII in Db major) [10]
- VI major (instead of VIm): "Sir Duke" chorus changes G#m to G major — borrowing from parallel major, major VI in place of diatonic VIm [11]

**Secondary dominants with sus4 coloring:**
Wonder systematically replaces secondary dominants with dominant 9(sus4) quality — achieves the harmonic function without accidentals. This gives his progressions a "cleaner" sound that works in pop context while still implying the underlying jazz tension [10][12].

**Extended harmony in pop/soul context:**
"Isn't She Lovely": vi–ii–V–I = C#m7–F#9–B9sus4–Emaj7. The ii chord (F#9) functions as V of V (secondary dominant), the V chord uses 9sus4 quality [10].

**Core progressions:**
```
"Isn't She Lovely": vi–ii(9)–V9sus4–Imaj7
"My Cherie Amour": I–IVsus–bVII–Vsus–V9
"I Wish": i–IV7 (Dorian vamp)
"Sir Duke" pre-chorus: IV9–bIII9–bII9–II9–...–bV9 resolves to V9 (chromatic)
"Ribbon in the Sky": ii–iii–VIsus
```

**Harmonically dense songs to study:**
1. "As" — modally complex, extended voicings throughout
2. "Sir Duke" — chromatic parallelism most visible here
3. "Pastime Paradise" — F–Dm–E7–Am modal ambiguity (E7 is borrowed V/vi, creates minor tonality feel within major key)
4. "All Day Sucker" — complex reharmonization, underanalyzed
5. "Golden Lady" — frequently cited by analysts for harmonic sophistication [13]

**Key resource:**
- "Groove and Flow: Six Analytical Essays on the Music of Stevie Wonder" (academia.edu) — access requires login but appears to be the primary academic source [13]
- Hooktheory tabs for "Sir Duke," "I Wish," "As" have verified crowd-sourced harmonic analysis [14]

### D'Angelo

**Overall approach:**
D'Angelo is the first R&B artist consistently described as writing songs with "jazz standard sophistication" recontextualized for post-hip-hop production [15]. His harmonic language is dense with extensions but delivered through groove and feel rather than explicit harmonic motion — "meaning lives in what's between beats" [16].

**Voodoo era (2000):**
Defining move: intentional metric ambiguity. Questlove and Pino Palladino's rhythm tracks deliberately slide against the grid; the chord harmony is relatively static so the rhythmic tension does the work. Chord progressions are simple but the voicings are dense [16].

- "Brown Sugar" (1995): Em7–A7–Bm7–F7. The F7 is the outlier — functions as a tritone sub or chromatic neighbor. The F7#9 (F–A–C–Eb–Ab, "Hendrix chord" — stacked major 3rd and minor 3rd above the dominant) is the signature voicing [17].
- "Chicken Grease": Built around minor 9th chords played in 16th note patterns — Prince's "chicken grease" technique applied to neo-soul production [18].
- "Untitled (How Does It Feel)": D Mixolydian. Chords: Dadd9–A7sus4–G6–C9 (main); bridge uses F#m7(9)–B7(9)–Em9–Asus. Well above average in chord complexity and novelty per Hooktheory analysis [19].

**Black Messiah era (2014):**
- Extended chords throughout: 4-5 note chords extending up to 7th, 9th, 11th, 13th — but D'Angelo implies rather than states the extensions (walking bass lines fill in the harmony) [20].
- Gospel-quartet choral tradition influence: stacked vocal harmonies implying extended chords in the same way a jazz piano voicing would [16].
- "1000 Deaths": builds on "Chicken Grease" rhythmic vocabulary; adds a "killer chord change" — not transcribed in public sources but described as the resolution that was "always missing" from the earlier track [20].
- "Really Love": jazz-adjacent groove, Questlove half-time rhythm + Palladino even bass notes; most "jazz standard" feeling track in his catalog [20].

**The D'Angelo dominant substitution:**
The 13sus4 chord is his dominant of choice. Rather than resolving V7 → I, he uses V13sus4 which sounds similar to a bVII maj chord or a IVm11 depending on voicing — creating tonal ambiguity that never fully resolves. This is the "no closure" harmonic quality his music is famous for [21].

**Harmonic fingerprint rules:**
- Rarely resolves dominant tension conventionally
- Minor 9th as default minor chord color
- 7b9 and 7#9 ("Hendrix chord") as chromatic color within otherwise static progressions
- Chord clusters: 9th, 3rd, 11th played in close proximity
- Groove is the primary harmonic engine — chords sustain while rhythm creates the tension arc

**Core progressions:**
```
"Brown Sugar": i7–IV7–vi7–bII7 (tritone sub or chromatic neighbor)
"Untitled": I(add9)–V7sus4–IV6–bVII9 [D Mixolydian]
"Chicken Grease" template: im9 vamp with 16th note rhythmic comping
Neo-soul dominant pattern: im11 → bVIImaj9 → IV13sus4 (cyclic, no functional resolution)
```

**Transcription/analysis resources:**
- Hooktheory: "Untitled (How Does It Feel)" has full chord analysis [19]
- Ultimate Guitar verified tabs for Voodoo-era tracks
- Red Bull Music Academy D'Angelo lecture (video, primary source interview) [22]
- ⚠ No comprehensive academic transcription book exists — gap in the literature

### Leon Thomas III

**Context:**
Thomas (b. 1993, Brooklyn) is primarily a songwriter/producer/vocalist rather than an instrumentalist-theorist. His harmonic fingerprint is best understood through his production choices and the tracks he writes for others, not through a developed solo guitar/piano vocabulary [23].

**MUTT (2024 album) — his most harmonically studied work:**
- "MUTT" (title track): F minor. Chords: Fm7–Bbm7–Ebmaj–Cmaj. Complexity score 95/100 on Hooktheory. Uses m11 and m9 reductions in production layers [24].
- "Vibes Don't Lie": Bb minor. Chords: Bbm7–Ebm7–Gbmaj7–F7. Classic i–iv–bVI–V pattern with extended voicings on each chord [25].

**Production environment his vocals live in:**
- Jazz-influenced harmonic foundation (cites Art Blakey, Miles Davis as influences) combined with neo-soul and rock texture [24]
- Selective vocal layering: pitching up to choir-like stacks that imply chords beyond what's played — same technique as D'Angelo's implied extensions [23]
- Guitar-driven grooves with off-beat percussion [26]

**Harmonic language in his written/produced work:**
- "Snooze" (SZA, co-written with Babyface): built from a simple idea expanded in the room — reportedly a gentle, suspended-feeling progression. The Babyface connection suggests gospel-soul ii-V-I foundations [27].
- His writing for Victoria Monet's *Jaguar* (2020): lush orchestration, warm extended chords in the neo-soul jazz crossover zone. Specific chord analysis not publicly available.
- General pattern in his production: prefers the m7–m9 spectrum on minor chords, prefers maj7–maj9 on major chords, avoids bare triads.

**Harmonic fingerprint:**
Thomas operates in the same extended-chord zone as D'Angelo and Wonder but with a cleaner, less ambiguous resolution tendency. Where D'Angelo avoids closure, Thomas leans into it — more conventional ii-V-I resolutions appear, but with extended voicing color throughout. The "Hendrix chord" (7#9) appears in his production less than D'Angelo; he favors smoother dominant colors (13sus4, 9sus4).

**Core progressions:**
```
"MUTT": im7–IVm7–bVImaj–VImaj (F minor: Fm7–Bbm7–Ebmaj–Cmaj)
"Vibes Don't Lie": im7–IVm7–bVImaj7–V7 (Bb minor: Bbm7–Ebm7–Gbmaj7–F7)
General Thomas template: im9 → bVImaj9 → IVm11 → V13sus4 (cyclic)
```

**Note on "classic" Leon Thomas (1936–1999):**
The research request appears to reference Leon Thomas III (b. 1993). The older Leon Thomas (jazz vocalist, Pharoah Sanders collaborator) is a different artist — his harmonic context was avant-garde jazz/spiritual jazz. Flag this distinction if Scales.sin content references "Leon Thomas" without disambiguation.

## Shared Harmonic Vocabulary (The Intersection)

These chord types appear across all four artists:

| Chord Type | Pass | Wonder | D'Angelo | Thomas |
|------------|------|--------|----------|--------|
| min7 | Base chord | Base chord | Base chord | Base chord |
| min9 | Yes | Yes | Yes (primary) | Yes (primary) |
| min11 | Yes | Yes (modal) | Yes | Yes |
| maj7 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| maj9 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| dom13sus4 | Yes (approach) | Yes (cadence) | Yes (dominant sub) | Yes (dominant sub) |
| dom7b9 | Yes | Secondary | Yes (#9 version) | Less common |
| Chromatic passing chords | Yes (approach) | Yes (Sir Duke) | Yes (Brown Sugar bII7) | Less prominent |
| Dorian/Mixolydian vamps | Yes (standards) | Yes (primary) | Yes (primary) | Yes |

**The absolute intersection** — chords that appear in all four:
- **m9**: The shared default minor chord color
- **maj9**: The shared default major chord color
- **dom13sus4** (or 9sus4): The shared "open dominant" that delays or avoids resolution
- **Dorian i–IV vamp** as a structural template

## Distinguishing Each Artist's Fingerprint

**Joe Pass:** The only artist who uses functional ii-V-I as a structural spine rather than a vamp context. His extended harmony is always in service of voice-leading to a resolution. Tritone sub is explicit and structural. Drop 2/3 voicings with melody on top. Bebop-rooted.

**Stevie Wonder:** The chromatic parallelism (passing 9th chords) is uniquely his. The IV→IVm borrowed chord move and the dominant 9sus4 as secondary dominant replacement are signature. He works in identifiable keys with surprise deviations — not modal stasis, but tonal centers with modal color borrowed in.

**D'Angelo:** The only artist who uses rhythmic dislocation as the primary harmonic tension mechanism. Chord progressions are often simple (2-3 chords); complexity lives in groove, voice leading implied by bass, and vocal harmony extensions. The dom7#9 ("Hendrix chord") appears nowhere else in this group with the same frequency.

**Leon Thomas:** The most conventional harmonic vocabulary of the four — but with the densest production texture layered over it. His signature is choir-like vocal stacking that implies chords the instruments aren't playing. Closest to Stevie Wonder in approach (functional harmony + extended voicing color), closest to D'Angelo in groove texture.

## Chord Progression Generator Rules (Style Templates)

### "In the style of Joe Pass"
```
Constraint: Always include a ii chord before V.
Use tritone sub: replace V7 with bII7 on repeat.
Dominant voicing: always add b9 or #11 (melodic minor scale from semitone above).
Resolution: always fully cadential — no suspended dominants that don't resolve.
Example: Dm9 | G13b9 | Cmaj9 | (repeat with Db9 as tritone sub for G13b9)
```

### "In the style of Stevie Wonder"
```
Constraint: Root in Dorian or Mixolydian. Two-chord vamp as base.
Spice 1: Replace V7 with V9sus4 — never use bare dominant 7th in cadence.
Spice 2: Insert bVII major chord as passing chord from IV back to I.
Spice 3: On chorus/bridge, substitute VIm with VI major (parallel major borrow).
Chromatic option: Insert descending chromatic passing 9th chords as pre-chorus device.
Example (Dorian): im9 | IVmaj7 | im9 | bVIImaj9–V9sus4 ||
```

### "In the style of D'Angelo"
```
Constraint: 2-3 chords max. Cyclic, no resolution.
Default minor chord: m9 with #9 color available.
Default dominant: 13sus4 or 7#9 (not plain 7th).
Rule: if progression repeats more than 4 bars, it's correct.
Rhythmic instruction: displace chord hits to beat 2.5 or 4+.
Example: im9 | bVIImaj9 | IV13sus4 | (loop)
Alternative: im9 | bII7#9 | im9 (two-chord chromatic color version)
```

### "In the style of Leon Thomas"
```
Constraint: im7 → IVm7 → bVImaj7 → V7 or V13sus4 (four-chord functional loop).
Default minor chord: m9.
Default major chord: maj7.
Dominant: 13sus4 preferred over plain V7; resolves to i (more conventional than D'Angelo).
Vocal implication: stack m9 chord, voice maj9 against it in upper register.
Example: im9 | IVm9 | bVImaj9 | V13sus4–im9 ||
```

## Watch Out For

1. **Leon Thomas disambiguation** — Two musicians named Leon Thomas. The younger (Thomas III, b. 1993) is the neo-soul producer/vocalist. The elder (1936-1999) is a jazz vocalist who collaborated with Pharoah Sanders. If Scales.sin includes either, label explicitly.

2. **"Dorian vamp" ≠ "Aeolian vamp"** — Wonder's "I Wish" and "Superstition" are Dorian (raised 6th gives the IV chord a major quality). This is the pedagogically critical distinction. Natural minor (Aeolian) gives you a iv chord; Dorian gives you IV major. The difference in sound is enormous. Do not conflate.

3. **D'Angelo's extensions are implied, not voiced** — His piano/guitar parts often play simpler voicings; the bass and vocal harmonics complete the extended chord picture. Analyzing just the piano/guitar chart will undercount the harmony. Any Scales.sin transcription should account for the full arrangement.

4. **Joe Pass's system is guitar-specific** — Drop 2/3 voicings, six-string range constraints, and approach chord fingerings are guitar technique. The harmonic concepts translate to any instrument, but the voicing shapes do not. Scales.sin visualizations should abstract to interval structure, not guitar-specific grids.

5. **"Sus4 dominant" vs "sus2 dominant"** — These are not interchangeable. V13sus4 (with 4th replacing the 3rd) is the neo-soul dominant replacement. V9sus2 appears in Wonder's ballads as an ambiguous tonal color. Distinguish when teaching.

6. **The "Hendrix chord" (7#9) is enharmonically ambiguous** — E7#9 contains both G# (major 3rd) and Bb (minor 3rd/augmented 2nd) simultaneously. This is why it sounds "dissonant but stable" — it implies both major and minor quality. Teaching this chord requires explaining the enharmonic tension, not just the formula.

7. ⚠ May be stale: The academic analysis of D'Angelo (Young-Soo Kim thesis exists for Joe Pass but not D'Angelo). No equivalent published transcription book for D'Angelo or Leon Thomas III exists as of research date. If Claude finds one, treat as new information.

"Before we start on the harmonic reference layer for Scales.sin, I had Javelin research the harmonic language of Joe Pass, Stevie Wonder, D'Angelo, and Leon Thomas III in depth. The shared DNA across all four is: minor 9 as the default minor chord color, dom13sus4 as the preferred dominant replacement (avoiding functional resolution), and Dorian/Mixolydian two-chord vamps as the primary structural template — this is what 'sounds like soul/jazz' regardless of which artist you're studying.
